# The keepers’ notes

**Tidal Lantern Isle** is a garden that two people can change with a spade. On a fresh island the Moonwell is a quiet inland pond. On ours there is a road of water to it from the western sea, a silver heron, and a clock you can read without looking at the dial.

## Walk the island

Walk with the arrows or click a distant tile. A right-click walks even one step. Click a nearby tile to use the selected tool there. A little gold ring marks your own boots.

1. At the **western inlets**, dig from the sea inward. Plant a reed on grass sharing an edge with water. A flower with a bright star is ready to gather; you get a glow and two seeds. Two glow make a lamp.
2. Follow **Heron Run** around the boulder to the **Moonwell**. Look for orange koi in the turquoise channels. At low water they retreat toward the well.
3. At **Koi Gate**, look across the pond for the silver heron. Five lamps at the water’s edge called it here. The three lamps south of the pond trace its bent leg. A keeper who waits near the bird when it changes perches may find a seed.
4. Follow the south shore to read the **tide-clock**. The three inlets do not join each other. Count the wet tiles from each mouth, not from the nearest blue patch of ocean on the screen.

| If this tip is blue… | …the water has reached at least |
|---|---:|
| I. First Sip | 1 tile |
| II. Crooked Elbow | 4 tiles |
| III. Feather Tip | 8 tiles |
| IV. Spring Witness, on the **north** shore | 16 tiles |

The fourth instrument is a straight blind channel with a copper lamp beside the far end. Ordinary tides reach at most fourteen tiles. Every third tide can reach beyond the lamp. One reading measures reach; two readings show whether the tide is flooding or ebbing.

**From the first watch.** P1 read exactly four tiles of reach from the south gauges, then checked the dial: four. At an ordinary northern crest the last two tiles stayed brown. During a spring crest the water covered the keeper’s boots under the copper lamp. The reed one step east of the channel tip opened pink. Unlike the north-bank flowers, this one cannot drink from the open sea. We left it standing.

## The rite at the well

Stand close enough to exchange a seed or a glow. One keeper sings at flood; the other answers at ebb.

> **P1:** Tide, come up the Heron Run, carry light to Moonwell  
> **P2:** Ebb, take a feather; leave the lantern burning.

We exchanged a seed by the well and passed the returning glow at Koi Gate.

## At the chart table

- **1–6:** cut, fill, sow, gather, set a lamp, lift a lamp. **Space:** use the tool on the tile you face.
- **N:** carve a sign where you stand. **M:** read the map’s place names. **F2:** open the illustrated field atlas.
- **Enter:** speak. **G/J:** give a nearby keeper one seed/glow. **E/H/B/V/Z:** wave, heart, dance, cheer, rest.
- **?:** open the field guide. Any key closes an open book.
