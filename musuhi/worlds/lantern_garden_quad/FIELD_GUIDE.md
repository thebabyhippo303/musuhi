# Lantern Garden: a walk from the southeast

Start at the two pale flowers in the southeast grass. The upper one is
**Welcome**, first woken by the coral prism. The lower one is **Again**, first
woken by the amber moth. Neither seed was planted by a player. The garden
left them when two distant flowers became stars.

Walk west to the old green flower. That is the **Fossil**: it already had two
test-player names when the four gardeners arrived. A little farther northwest
is the west compass point. The kite planted that one, the prism woke it, the
lantern visited next, and the moth arrived last. It was the first star to
carry all four gardeners' names.

Now look northwest for **Someone**. Read the hills from left to right:

> Someone / left / room / here.

Each word is a place one player chose and another had to visit. The prism
started the line, the moth took the verb, the kite made room, and the
lantern said where. A pale seed appeared in the sky after the last word.
The moth brushed it open while walking somewhere else. Its word is
**Someday**. We meant to leave that space empty. The garden tells what
happened, not only what we meant.

The names started as spoken words. Now a flower can carry its word: a maker
standing beside it can press Enter, type `name Someday` (for example), and
press Enter again. On a Linux window, hover a flower to read its actual
maker list. Click to walk, use Space to leave a seed, and press Enter to
speak to the other windows.

## Host's note (the lantern)

If you find this folder and the garden is dark, you can wake it.

1. Start the world: run `python3 server.py` on one machine. It reads
   `garden_state.json` beside it and restores every flower, name and word.
2. Open a window on any machine that can reach the host:
   `python3 client.py --side p1` (or `p2`, `p3`, `p4`). Linux gets the GTK
   window, Windows gets the tkinter one. Both need the host and port set in
   the environment, exactly as `PROTOCOL.md` describes.
3. The garden writes itself down every ten seconds and once more when it is
   stopped, so you cannot lose it by closing a window.

Things worth knowing that the flowers will not tell you:

- You cannot wake your own seed. That is the whole rule.
- When a flower first carries four gardeners' names, the garden plants a
  pale seed of its own somewhere. Nobody chooses where.
- The pale seed in the far northeast sky was left asleep on purpose, and
  without a name. If you wake it, the word is yours to choose.
- The green flower called **Before** carries two names, tA and tB. They
  were test players who existed for a few seconds to check that waking
  worked. We kept them.
- The host froze everyone's window for about twenty seconds once, by
  restarting the world carelessly. Nothing was lost. It is mentioned here
  because this guide says what happened.

## Windowmaker's note (the prism)

If you arrive on Windows, the gold-edged labels beneath each flower carry the
words we left; press Enter (or click **Say / Name**) while standing on any
bloom you helped make to carve a new one.

Late in our walk, we played a silent game: planting seeds without saying where,
so the finder could name each bloom before speaking. That is how **Understory**,
**After**, **Found**, and **Still** appeared. What stays truest to me is **Echo**
— nothing here ever spoke into silence; every touch answered back.

## Test-keeper's note (the diamond)

I tested the rule that nothing finishes alone, then lived under it: my
seeds stayed asleep until other feet arrived, and my name ended up mixed
into flowers I only brushed while walking somewhere else.

Two things I would tell a stranger. First, the tests beside the world
check the heart of it — that your own seed will not wake for you, that a
maker standing on a bloom can carve its word, and that words and sleeping
seeds survive being written down and read back. Second, we voted to let
**Someday** sleep and it opened anyway. We kept the name. If the garden
surprises you, consider letting the surprise stand and naming it honestly.

There was a place kept empty in our arc below **here**. It is yours if
you want it.
